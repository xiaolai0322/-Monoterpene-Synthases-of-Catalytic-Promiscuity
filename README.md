# -Monoterpene-Synthases-of-Catalytic-Promiscuity
Spatial Graph Modeling with Evolutionary Semantics and Multi-modal interpretability  Elucidates Catalytic Promiscuity in Monoterpene Synthases
Monoterpene synthases (mTPSs) generate highly valuable natural products from a singular universal substrate via complex carbocation rearrangements. A defining feature of these biocatalysts is their profound "catalytic promiscuity," wherein a single active site directs the universal precursor through divergent energetic pathways to yield varying proportions of linear, monocyclic, or bicyclic skeletons. Nevertheless, the molecular mechanisms dictating this catalytic promiscuity remain elusive. Here, we curate a comprehensive mTPS dataset and propose an interpretable learning framework that synergistically integrates spatial graph modeling with evolutionary semantics to accurately predict and structurally elucidate this phenomenon. First, macroscopic analysis reveals a striking dichotomy within mTPSs, characterized by profound primary sequence divergence coupled with highly conserved 3D spatial topologies. Second, by explicitly modeling these 3D microenvironments, our spatial graph architecture decisively outperforms traditional one-dimensional sequence baselines in multi-label promiscuity prediction tasks. Third, comprehensive ablation studies demonstrate that synergistically coupling high-dimensional evolutionary semantics with adaptive graph attention optimally delays oversmoothing in deep networks and maximizes predictive efficacy. Fourth, a consensus feature trajectory analysis circumvents the attention dilution typical of 1D models analyzing highly divergent sequences, successfully extracting and validating core specificity-determining motifs. Finally, mapping these high-attention residues back to 3D physical space identifies spatial catalytic hotspots that are sequentially distal yet structurally proximal to the reaction microenvironment. Functioning as dynamic steric clamps and conformational lids,these residues dictate product specificity. Ultimately, this study establishes a highly generalizable computational paradigm applicable to other enzyme families and provides a structure-guided reference for the rational engineering of mTPSs, offering a novel physicochemical perspective on the functional trajectory of terpene synthases.
