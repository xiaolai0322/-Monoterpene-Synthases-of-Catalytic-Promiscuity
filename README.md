# -Monoterpene-Synthases-of-Catalytic-Promiscuity
Spatial Graph Modeling with Evolutionary Semantics and Multi-modal interpretability  Elucidates Catalytic Promiscuity in Monoterpene Synthases


Monoterpene synthases (mTPSs) generate highly valuable natural products from a singular universal substrate via complex carbocation rearrangements. A defining feature of these biocatalysts is their profound catalytic promiscuity, whereby a single active pocket directs the universal precursor through divergent energetic trajectories to yield varying proportions of linear, monocyclic, or bicyclic skeletal frameworks. Nevertheless, computational approaches to resolve mTPS promiscuity remain noticeably lacking, and the molecular mechanisms dictating this phenomenon remain elusive. To address this challenge, we curate a comprehensive mTPS dataset and propose a spatial graph modeling framework that synergistically integrates deep evolutionary semantics and multi-modal interpretability. This framework utilizes evolutionary context to empower superior predictive accuracy while leveraging structural explainability to precisely pinpoint critical catalytic determinants, thereby enabling both the efficient prediction and structural elucidation of mTPS catalytic promiscuity.Although primary mTPS sequences exhibit profound divergence, their highly conserved global three-dimensional topologies indicate that the key determinants governing catalytic promiscuity are embedded within localized spatial microenvironments, establishing a strong biological premise for deploying 3D graph learning. Rigorous baseline comparisons demonstrate that structure-aware graph neural networks decisively outperform traditional 1D sequence baselines across multiple stringent multi-label metrics, demonstrating that explicit 3D geometric constraints are indispensable for the accurate prediction of multi-product catalytic specificities. Furthermore, comprehensive ablation studies uncover an optimal coupling paradigm between high-dimensional ESM-2 evolutionary semantics and graph attention networks. This specific architecture effectively mitigates the oversmoothing phenomenon to successfully capture the localized microenvironments and long-range spatial interaction networks that regulate reaction branching.To address the attention dilution bottleneck inherent to 1D sequence attributions when processing highly divergent datasets, we introduce a structure-rooted interpretability approach that precisely maps algorithmic weights onto conserved physical scaffolds. This strategy visually elucidates an evolutionary landscape driven by the synergy of feature inheritance and novel domain recruitment across the distinct enzyme groups. Taken together, this structure-rooted, spatially intelligent computing paradigm not only deepens the molecular understanding of complex enzymatic evolutionary mechanisms but also delivers a powerful digital blueprint for the rational design and precise engineering of universal biocatalysts tailored for synthetic biology.
